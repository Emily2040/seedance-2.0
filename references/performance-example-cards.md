# Performance and dialogue: four directing choices

These authored English-language cards teach different ways to stage expression, timing and speech. All are ungenerated. Durations are planning assumptions, not measured line timing or platform limits. Verify active-surface controls before choosing settings; no card authorizes a generation. Use the Director's Read to preserve the requested observation/performance instead of adding conflict to fill a narrative record.

## Card: Land beyond the chalk line

**Evidence:** Concept; not generated or inspected.

**Brief:** A joyful adult dancer plays with a chalk line in an empty courtyard. Keep the full body visible, normal speed and open expression; no competitive or recovery story. The line and courtyard are authored.

**Choice:** Keep a held wide frame while the dancer crosses it. Following the dancer would bring us closer but lose the fixed line as a spatial reference; choose the visible crossing.

**Bindings:** None. T2V with an original fictional performer; no likeness reference.

**Settings:** T2V; plan eight seconds, horizontal frame, normal speed. Model, tier and resolution are unselected.

**Why this direction:** The unmoving chalk line makes the playful footwork and final landing readable without forcing a restrained facial gesture.

**Prompt:**

```text
Hold a wide eye-level view of an adult dancer in loose orange trousers beside a single white chalk line across a sunlit courtyard. At normal speed, the dancer takes two quick sideways steps, hops across the line and lands with both feet on the far side, arms opening into a broad smile. Keep the whole body and chalk line visible throughout. Sound: shoe scuffs, the landing and a short laugh. Hold the final stance long enough to see both feet settle.
```

**Check:** The dancer crosses the same line once; both feet land on the far side; the full body remains in frame; energy and normal speed survive. Reject an invented fall, sadness or victory cutaway.

**Fallback:** If the hop breaks contact or anatomy, propose a step across the line while preserving the speed and smile. It loses the airborne accent; use only if the user accepts that change.

## Card: The feather wins the pause

**Evidence:** Concept; not generated or inspected.

**Brief:** A physical comedy demonstration: a performer tries to blow a feather into a dustpan, but it floats back onto the performer's nose. The joke is the unexpected landing and pause, not humiliation or hidden motives.

**Choice:** Frame the face, feather and dustpan together and let the action finish. A feather close-up would clarify its path but conceal the performer's stillness; choose the shared view.

**Bindings:** None. T2V with a fictional adult and invented props.

**Settings:** T2V; plan eight seconds, held medium-wide frame. Model, tier and resolution are unselected.

**Why this direction:** One uninterrupted view is intended to let the feather's landing provide the comic endpoint without an explanatory reaction cut.

**Prompt:**

```text
Hold a medium-wide three-quarter view of an adult at a wooden table, one white feather near the edge and a blue dustpan held just below it. The performer leans in and gives one small puff. The feather rises, drifts back toward the face and rests across the tip of the performer's nose. The performer freezes with the dustpan still waiting, then lowers their gaze toward the feather. Soft window light, no camera move. Sound: the puff and quiet room tone through the pause; no laugh track.
```

**Check:** Track the same feather from table to nose with no teleporting or extra feather; the empty dustpan stays below the edge; the pause comes after landing. The sound must not announce the joke early.

**Fallback:** If the feather path fails, propose a practical prop shot or an editorial insert of the landing. The insert sacrifices the uninterrupted view that proves the path; do not call the revised effect equivalent.

## Card: Say the line, then let the clock answer

**Evidence:** Concept; not generated or inspected.

**Brief:** An adult clock repairer demonstrates a working clock and says exactly, “That's the first time it's kept time.” No second speaker, added backstory or altered wording. The performer, bench and clock are authored.

**Choice:** Keep a stable medium close-up through the line, then hold for the ticking. An insert during speech would show the mechanism but prevent continuous mouth review; choose the uninterrupted speaker.

**Bindings:** None. T2V with an original fictional voice and performer; no real-person voice imitation.

**Settings:** T2V with generated speech only if supported; plan eight seconds. Read the exact line at the intended pace before committing timing. Model, tier and resolution are unselected.

**Why this direction:** Separating the spoken turn from the final tick is intended to make both speech accuracy and the demonstration audible.

**Prompt:**

```text
Hold a medium close-up of an adult repairer seated beside a small brass clock on a workbench. Keep the face unobscured and the head steady. Looking toward the clock, the repairer says clearly, "That's the first time it's kept time." The hands rest on the bench during the line. After speaking, the repairer smiles openly and listens. Soft bench-lamp light. Sound: the exact spoken line once, quiet room tone and the clock ticking through the final hold; no music or additional voice.
```

**Check:** Compare every spoken word with the exact line; inspect mouth timing independently of audio quality; ensure one speaker and an audible hold after the line. No captions are assumed to repair incorrect speech.

**Fallback:** If the line is clipped, propose a longer supported duration or a separate approved voice recording and post workflow. Preserve the exact words unless the user authorizes a rewrite; do not generate extra takes automatically.

## Card: Watch the chair cover settle

**Evidence:** Concept; not generated or inspected.

**Brief:** A quiet observation of a blue cotton cover moving on an empty balcony chair. No absent-person story, grief cue or symbolic reveal. The setting is invented.

**Choice:** Hold enough of the chair and rail to see the breeze act on the fabric. A macro view would emphasize weave but hide how the cover hangs; choose the spatial view.

**Bindings:** None. T2V; no claim that a real location was filmed.

**Settings:** T2V; plan six seconds, horizontal frame. Model, tier and resolution are unselected.

**Why this direction:** A small change followed by settling gives the observation an endpoint without manufacturing a dramatic turn.

**Prompt:**

```text
Hold a still medium view of an empty wooden balcony chair with a blue cotton cover draped over its back. In soft overcast daylight, a breeze lifts the loose lower corner away from the chair leg, then lets it fall against the wood. Keep the chair, rail and fabric pattern unchanged. Sound: fabric brushing wood and distant leaves moving, no music or speech. End on the cover resting against the leg while the leaves remain audible.
```

**Check:** The same corner lifts and settles; the cover does not become a different garment or pattern; no person or melancholy score is added. Preserve the quiet duration rather than demanding a reveal.

**Fallback:** If the fabric morphs, reduce the lift or use a captured cloth reference with permission. A still image can preserve the material but loses the moving observation; state that tradeoff.
